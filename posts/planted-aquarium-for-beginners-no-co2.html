# Planted Aquarium for Beginners: Complete Setup Guide (No CO2)

You do not need a CO2 system, expensive substrate, or a degree in chemistry to grow a lush planted aquarium. A low-tech planted tank — no pressurized CO2, modest light, and easy plants — is the most reliable way for a beginner to get a green, thriving aquarium that stays stable for years.

We've set up dozens of low-tech planted tanks across every common tank size. This guide covers exactly what you need, what you can skip, and the mistakes that cause most beginners to give up.

Why low-tech planted tanks work for beginners

A low-tech (no-CO2) planted tank trades a little growth speed for a lot of stability. Plants grow more slowly without injected CO2, which means:

  • Algae has a harder time outcompeting them
  • You do not have to obsess over fertilizer dosing
  • If you miss a water change, the tank forgives you

The trade-off is simple: choose plants with low light and low nutrient demands, and pace your expectations. A tank that grows steadily for two years beats one that grows fast for three months and then crashes into an algae bloom.

Tank, filter, and light requirements

Start with the largest tank your space and budget allow. A 10-gallon (40 L) tank is the practical minimum; 20 gallons (75 L) gives you far more room for error with water chemistry. A solid starter aquarium kit with a lid and light is the lazy-compatible option — everything fits, nothing leaks.

  • Filter: any hang-on-back (HOB) filter rated for your tank volume works. Canister filters are nicer but not required at this stage.
  • Light: you need a grow light with a full spectrum — not just the white LED strip that ships with many kits. The NICREW ClassicLED or a comparable full-spectrum bar is the budget standard for low-tech tanks.
  • Heater: set to 72–78°F (22–26°C). Most easy plants and fish do fine anywhere in that range.

Best beginner plants (no CO2 needed)

These are the plants that grow with nothing but a light and the nutrients already in your tank. They are also the most forgiving of beginner mistakes:

  • Anubias (any species) — the indestructible classic. Glue or tie it to wood or rock; never bury the rhizome.
  • Java fern — same story: attach to hardscape, not substrate.
  • Cryptocoryne (crypts) — slow to settle, then bulletproof. Expect them to melt back at first; that is normal and they regrow.
  • Java moss — fills in hardscape and gives shrimp somewhere to hide.
  • Vallisneria — fast background grass that grows like weeds once rooted.
  • Hornwort or water sprite — floating or free-floating plants that soak up excess nutrients and shade the water (algae hates this).

Avoid "carpeting" plants like dwarf baby tears without CO2 — they will be a constant frustration. If you want a low foreground carpet, try dwarf sagittaria or a Monte Carlo mat, and expect it to grow slowly.

Substrate and planting basics

The substrate myth is that you need an expensive planted-tank soil. In a low-tech tank, an aquarium soil like Fluval Stratum or ADA Amazonia makes life easier (it buffers water and holds nutrients), but plain sand or gravel with root tabs works too.

  • Add root tabs beneath heavy root feeders (crypts, sagittaria) every 2–3 months.
  • Layer soil at ~2 inches (5 cm) minimum for rooted plants.
  • Do not rinse soil — it will cloud briefly, then settle in a day or two.

When planting, use long tweezers to push each stem or root clump individually. Do not shove a whole bundle of stems into one hole — that is the classic planted-tank mistake, and the buried stems rot.

Lighting schedule and fertilizer essentials

Low-tech tanks run on an 6–8 hour light cycle. More light does not mean more growth without CO2 — it means more algae.

  • Start at 6 hours/day and increase to 8 only if your plants are growing well and algae is not.
  • A timer makes the schedule automatic; it is the single cheapest piece of gear that improves results.
  • Fertilizer: a complete all-in-one liquid fertilizer (Easy Green or a comparable all-in-one) dosed once or twice a week is plenty. Skip iron-only or potassium-only bottles — beginners do not need to dose macros individually in a low-tech tank.

Water changes are your real fertilizer safety net: 30% weekly. If you see algae, first cut light hours and feed less — do not reach for chemicals.

FAQ

How long until my planted tank looks full?

With fast plants like vallisneria and hornwort, you will see visible growth in 2–3 weeks; a "full" look takes 2–4 months.

Do I need CO2 for a planted tank?

No. Low-tech tanks grow a satisfying, healthy plant community without it. CO2 is for high-growth show tanks and carpets.

Why are my plants melting?

Melting after planting is normal for crypts and some emersed-grown plants. Give them 2–3 weeks; new leaves adapted to your water will replace the melted ones.

How do I stop algae?

Cut light to 6 hours, do weekly 30% water changes, and add fast-growing plants. Those three fixes resolve most beginner algae issues.

Can I keep shrimp with a planted tank?

Yes — planted tanks are ideal for neocaridina shrimp. They clean up detritus and their populations stay manageable in a 10–20 gallon tank.

Start simple: a 10–20 gallon tank, anubias and crypts, a full-spectrum light on a 6-hour timer, weekly water changes, and all-in-one fertilizer. That combination is the whole hobby — everything else is optional.
